TAMV Research Identity

The TRI is the network's internal identifier for membership, collaboration and academic traceability. This page defines its format, its roles, its stack of external identifiers, and with surgical precision what the cryptographic seal that accompanies it can and cannot prove.

01 — Definition

What a TRI is and what it is not

An identifier is only useful if someone else can resolve it and if its scope is bounded. These two lists are the bounding.

The TRI is

  • Internal identifier for membership, collaboration, affiliation and academic traceability.
  • A link between person, affiliation and output: profile, roles, projects, datasets, software, reviews.
  • A mechanism of institutional traceability and a register of verifiable contributions.
  • A tool of internal academic governance, with informed consent as a condition.
  • The anchor of the integrity seal when a proposal enters the review flow.

The TRI is not

  • A university degree, academic grade or professional certification.
  • Academic accreditation or a substitute for ORCID, ISNI, DOI or ROR.
  • An automatic guarantee of quality: email verification and identity verification are different things.
  • Authorization to claim authorship without real contribution.
  • Civil identity or immigration document.
02 — Format

Structure of the identifier

Legible, sequential and with no embedded personal information: the identifier must not reveal name, email or any sensitive datum.

TRI-TAMV-YYYY-XXXX

TRI    → TAMV Research Identity
TAMV   → Organización emisora
YYYY   → Año de alta institucional
XXXX   → Número correlativo único

Example of form: TRI-TAMV-2026-0001. It is an illustrative pattern of the specification, not the record of a registered enrollment.

TRI rules
  • Requires the member's informed consent to exist and for each published datum.
  • It may be corrected, updated or withdrawn at the holder's request.
  • It must be linked to public evidence whenever possible; a non-verifiable datum is marked as declared.
  • It grants no automatic authorship and does not replace external evaluation or peer review.
  • It is used together with ORCID, DOI and —when it exists— ROR, never in their place.

06 — Seal

The integrity seal, without mystery

The network computes a cryptographic digest over a canonical serialization of the dossier. Anyone can recompute it and compare.

objeto           : propuesta, dataset, software o documento
serialización    : orden canónico de campos, UTF-8, sin metadatos variables
algoritmo        : SHA-512 → resumen de contenido, no firma
digest publicado : 3f9c1e…<hex>        // ejemplo de forma, no un valor real
verificación     : un tercero recalcula el digest sobre los mismos bytes
                   y lo compara; la coincidencia prueba igualdad de contenido

What the seal does demonstrate

  • That this exact copy of the dossier was not altered relative to the one that was sealed.
  • That two different versions differ, without needing to read them in full.
  • That the version examined by a reviewer can be audited on any later date.
  • That the comparison is public: it does not require trusting a network server.

What the seal does NOT demonstrate

  • Authorship. A hash does not say who wrote anything; that is provided by ORCID or an external record.
  • Antiquity. Without an RFC 3161 timestamp from an authority, the digest does not prove that the document existed on a given date.
  • Non-repudiation. It is not a qualified electronic signature and has no legal validity in itself.
  • Originality. The same text uploaded by another person produces the same digest: a match does not resolve who was first without external temporal anchoring.
  • Truthfulness. Nothing cryptographic validates that the claims in the document are correct.
  • Irresistible presence. No technology embeds an affiliation inside another person's file in a way that cannot be removed.

These limits are documented in the project itself: the digest of a snapshot confirms that the bytes match under a declared serialization rule; it is not a signature, nor a trusted timestamp, nor a root of trust, nor a certification.

07 — Anchoring

Making traceability auditable by third parties

Here the proposal moves from "internal record" to evidence that can be checked without trusting TAMV. Each mechanism covers a different threat.

Anchoring in a third-party archive with deletion protectionDurable public presence of the dossierThe third party that archivesOptional, low cost

The combination that does work

Content digest + RFC 3161 timestamp + DOI of the deposit + author ORCID verified by OAuth. Four pieces, each with a different guarantor. That set is auditable; a hash alone, stored in the network's database, is not.

08 — Integrity

Originality and plagiarism: what the network can and cannot do

Plagiarism prevention is a problem of detection and of documentary consequence. Cryptography provides one piece, not the solution.

Achievable

  • An earlier public date: a DOI or an external timestamp establish that a version existed before.
  • A documented provenance chain among source, author, version and review.
  • An integrity filter at admission, which blocks detected plagiarism before publishing.
  • Withdrawal and public notice when a work turns out to be plagiarized or plagiarizes, with traceability of the change.
  • Verifiable contributions per person, which make visible whose is what.

Not achievable

  • Preventing someone from copying a public document: an identifier does not prevent unauthorized reproduction.
  • Detecting all plagiarism without access to comparable corpora or external similarity tools.
  • Substituting legal action for copyright: the network documents; the legal framework is determined by the applicable laws.
  • Turning the possession of a TRI into scientific authority.

Privacy and minimal data

The TRI stores only name, role, area, contact email, public identifiers, contributions and membership status. It does not store identity documents, home addresses, personal phone numbers, financial data or biometric information. Profiles are born private and are published only with express consent and approval. This site is static: it installs no analytics and no cookies. Data processing in the member register remains subject to legal review in line with the applicable Mexican legislation and the terms of the providers; the network does not declare certified compliance with any privacy framework.
