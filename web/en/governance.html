Governance, ethics and editorial policy

How decisions are made in TAMV Online Network and how far its authority reaches. This page separates explicitly what is incorporated today from what is written but unpopulated, and declares that its outputs are internal rulings: they are not third-party accreditation or certification.

02 — Principles

The fifteen canonical principles

List taken from section 2 of the VETRA Ω∞ v1.0 canon. These are the rules an internal ruling must be able to cite; they are not an accreditation nor a code of an external entity.

  1. Evidence before claim.
  2. Authorship and contribution declared with precision.
  3. Method before conclusion.
  4. Reproducibility proportional to the phase of the project.
  5. Security and privacy by design.
  6. Independence and conflicts of interest declared.
  7. Rights, licenses and consent before disclosure.
  8. Impact and potential harm considered together with benefits.
  9. Decisions recorded, reasoned and appealable.
  10. Final human oversight.
  11. Equal access to criteria; no fictitious equality of outcomes.
  12. Reassessment upon material changes or incidents.
  13. Limitations are published together with the conclusions.
  14. The absence of a datum is not turned into a claim of nonexistence.
  15. Fail-closed for critical non-compliances defined in advance.

What principle 11 means

All persons receive the same public criteria and the same right of correction and appeal. The framework does not promise identical outcomes: an honestly labeled prototype and a study with complete data do not occupy the same state of evidence.

What principle 15 means

If a critical control defined in advance fails, the process stops, is suspended or requires remediation, regardless of the aggregate score. An absent datum is not automatically a failure: it is classified as unknown, documented or not applicable with justification, and gaps are not silently scored as verified.

The canon's methodological references — the ALLEA Code of Conduct, COPE guidelines, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and FAIR principles — are design sources. The document itself clarifies that they do not imply affiliation, certification or endorsement of TAMV by their owners.

03 — Gates

The three mandatory gates of VETRA Ω∞

Each admission gate leaves a record. The author's self-assessment helps prepare the dossier, but is not equivalent to passing it, and the gates do not publish automatically: the dossier passes to human review. The full name of the framework is Verificación y Evaluación de Ciencia, Tecnología y Rendimiento Avanzado (Advanced Science, Technology and Performance Verification & Evaluation Framework).

Gate 1

Integrity, authorship and provenance

Objective: verify that the proposal has an identifiable origin, bounded claims and a minimum of transparency.

What it requires

  • Title, abstract, responsible person and object type.
  • Honest contributions and authorship, including substantive use of AI.
  • Primary sources and the pertinent state of the art.
  • Novelty expressed as a hypothesis, not as an undemonstrated fact.
  • Provenance of data, code, models, images and materials.
  • Funding, relevant relationships and declared conflicts.
  • Publication rights, proposed license and third-party permissions.
  • Explicit separation between demonstrated, preliminary, aspirational and outstanding.

What blocks it

Plagiarism or deliberate fabrication of data, falsified authorship, deliberately invented references, material concealment of conflicts, lack of authorization to disclose protected content or substantiated documentary fraud. Possible outcome: complete for review / requires information / blocked on integrity grounds.

Gate 2

Rigor, method and reproducibility

Objective: that another competent person can understand what was done, why, with what criteria, and how to evaluate it or repeat it.

What it requires

  • Problem and question, or a verifiable technological need.
  • Defined hypothesis or requirements and success criteria.
  • Method, design, architecture or procedure.
  • Comparison with relevant alternatives.
  • Data, code, configuration, versions or replication instructions when legal and possible.
  • Tests with source, environment, sample and limitations.
  • Register of errors, known failures and threats to validity.
  • A future reproducible plan if there are no results yet.

What blocks it

Empirical conclusions without sufficient data or method; benchmarks without procedure; superiority claims without a fair comparison; an unreconstructable result deceptively presented as proven. Possible outcome: method sufficient / methodological correction / major revision / not substantiated.

Gate 3

Responsibility, rights and impact

Objective: detect foreseeable harms, misuse, legal non-compliance, risks to privacy or to the rights of third parties, and unsustainable operations.

What it requires

  • User profile, context of use and prohibited uses.
  • Risk assessment, affected population and plausible harms.
  • Personal data, consent, minimization, access, retention, deletion and transfers.
  • Security controls, secrets management, threat model and incident response.
  • Licenses for software, data, models and content; prior-art review if patentability is claimed.
  • Ethics review or regulatory authorization where applicable.
  • Human oversight, appeal, shutdown or withdrawal, and a correction mechanism.
  • Operating cost, dependence on third parties and sustainability.

What blocks it

Risk of serious harm without credible mitigation; unauthorized use of sensitive data; a critical vulnerability exposed without treatment; illegal disclosure; a substantiated rights violation; absence of human oversight where it is critical; retaliation or intentional discrimination. Possible outcome: risk acceptable for the phase / conditional / suspend until mitigated / not fit for publication in its current form.

The gates are not the complete evaluation

After admission, the canon defines fourteen meta-evaluation domains — scientific validity, technical validation, security, privacy, ethics, reproducibility, governance, social impact, economic viability, integrity, cognitive reliability of AI, meta-evaluation, evidence and provenance, and future consequences — scored with a rubric from 0 to 4 and weights summing to 100. That numbering is a pilot hypothesis: the thresholds and bands (85–100, 70–84, 50–69, 0–49) are not a universal scientific standard, are not calibrated with historical data, and must be tested by measuring agreement between reviewers and error rates before being used for high-impact decisions. The average is never published alone: it is accompanied by scores by domain, evidence, disagreements and limits.

Planned decision states: received, requires minimal information, under independent evaluation, minor revision, major revision, accepted under conditions, rejected with reasons, suspended, withdrawn by the authorship or revoked when a cause that justifies it is demonstrated.

04 — AI and authority

Governance of Isabella Villaseñor AI

Isabella Villaseñor AI (GENESIS Ω∞ / UFCOS) is the ecosystem's related technology project. It is designed with bounded authority: it proposes and organizes evidence, and the final decision rests with responsible persons.

Axiom of separation of capabilities

Knowledge ≠ memory ≠ reasoning ≠ decision ≠ authority ≠ execution ≠ evidence ≠ impact ≠ learning.

No subsystem may silently assume the properties or the authority of another. Reasoning is not deciding; remembering is not being true; a cited record is not proof of the underlying claim; a decision is not a legal authorization; and a score is not a certification.

Role in VETRA
Analytical assistanceThe canon describes the system and its fourteen support agents as modules that organize sources, compare documents, propose questions and detect incoherencies. They have no human vote, cannot assign themselves authority and cannot generate certifications on their own.
Nature of the agents
Specified, not deployedThey are documented analysis modules; not necessarily fourteen models executable in production.
Mandatory label
AI-assistedEvery AI output is labeled as AI-assisted, links its evidence, includes uncertainty and is approved or corrected by a responsible human reviewer.
Deployment status
Outstanding independent testThe consolidation route sets that Isabella V6 Terminus be submitted to independent testing before broad deployment, and the roadmap requires independent review for the scope that is deployed.
What the system can do and what it cannot decide

Can: propose hypotheses, summarize sources, flag inconsistencies, structure questions, normalize metadata, compare versions, identify missing evidence and draft questions for the reviewer.

Cannot:

  • Convert an unchecked reference into evidence.
  • Authorize itself, approve its own product or certify external facts.
  • Invent references, authors, test results, signatures, reviewer identities or approvals.
  • Silently override a reviewer or the published policy.
  • Turn the absence of a record into proof of nonexistence.
  • Make legal, ethical, financial or publication decisions with material consequences without responsible human review.
  • Substitute for the responsibility of the human committee. The document states it in these terms: a convincing answer does not constitute evidence.

In addition, the framework itself declares that VETRA does not, on its own, authorize clinical, financial, labor, legal or critical-infrastructure use, and that the score of an evaluation does not automatically translate into market price, patent probability, promise of return or investment decision.

06 — Appeal

Appeal, conflicts and final human oversight

A decision that cannot be challenged is not a process; it is authority without a counterweight. Here is the described mechanism and its implementation state.

How to appeal

  1. The applicant submits a reasoned appeal: new evidence, factual error or procedural conflict.
  2. It is reviewed by persons other than those who made the initial decision, where feasible.
  3. The resolution is documented and, if the state changes, the reason and the date are recorded.
  4. A security incident, a falsification, a rights violation or material new evidence may suspend a prior decision, following a documented and proportional process.

What is declared as a conflict

  • Employment and financial relationships.
  • Kinship and tutorship.
  • Recent collaboration and commercial competition.
  • Any conflict reasonably perceived.

The secretariat records the declaration and assigns another reviewer when the conflict is material. Authors, funders and team members of the object do not control the ruling on it, and the founder cannot be sole judge of his own systems. The pilot metric puts it precisely: the absence of declarations does not prove the absence of conflict.

Final human oversight

The decision belongs to responsible persons, is reasoned and remains recorded. No numeric average substitutes deliberation or overrides a critical failure: if two reviews disagree materially, a clarification or a third ruling is requested. Discordant reviews are not necessarily bad; they may reveal real uncertainty, and what is required is to document how the contradiction was resolved, not to impose an artificial unanimity.

Non-compliances that are not compensated by a high average

  • Deliberately fabricated evidence, substantiated plagiarism or deceptive authorship.
  • Known serious risk without proportional mitigation.
  • Illegitimate treatment or publication of personal data.
  • Clear infringement of rights or absence of a fundamental permission.
  • An exploitable critical vulnerability that should prevent release.
  • Absence of human oversight for high-impact decisions.
  • Unmanaged conflict of interest that invalidates the review.
  • Attempt to manipulate reviewers or alter evidence.
Money and decisions, separated

The canon states the rule in these words: "A paid membership does not guarantee acceptance, more positive review, certification, ROR ID, publication or funding." Business services may carry fees for integration, hosting, support or technical evaluation, under separate contracts and with conflict declaration.

A rejection or a suspension must be reasoned, limited to what the evidence allows one to claim, and appealable.

Implementation state

The mechanism exists as written policy and as specified private tables. It is not publicly operating: there is no reviewer roster, there is no review administration interface, and there are no resolved appeals to show. The form can export a local draft while intake remains closed.

07 — Data

Data protection and privacy limits

The design is private by default. A member profile is not published by registering, by paying or by automatic approval from a system.

Operating rules of the member register

  • Every submitted profile starts as pending_review; the register does not automatically approve anyone and does not make a profile visible.
  • The public resolver returns only profiles with approved status, public visibility and express consent recorded as true.
  • The internal session identifier (Auth0 subject) is a private key and never appears in the public profile.
  • Deletion removes the profile and its identifier links and leaves a pseudonymous record of the withdrawn identifier — called TAMV ID in the platform code — to prevent reassignment; the identity in Auth0 is administered by that provider separately.
  • Mail verification is not verification of civil identity; declared ORCIDs or ISNIs are validated by format and checksum, and remain declared, not verified as belonging to their owner.
  • The tables maintain row-level access control and revoked public privileges; the service key is used only on the server.
  • No account is created with client-side JavaScript alone: a verified session, explicit consent and server-side validation are required.

Legal gate still deliberately closed

The registration API answers 503 while the real name of the controller, its address and a monitored privacy channel are lacking, and while the retention policy is not approved. This is deliberate: a production flow must not collect personal data when those references are placeholders. The same applies to VETRA proposal intake, whose configuration flags remain false until migration, security testing, retention and deletion are approved.

Data in the exploration tools

Pod and quarantine actions in the HCU prototype change only the state visible in the visitor's browser; they are not production controls nor persistent member records. Queries sent to the search endpoint must be short, non-sensitive terms.

Confidentiality of review

Reviewer identifiers, confidential material and personal data are not published without a legal basis and explicit policy. Use of confidential material for personal benefit is prohibited, and personal data or confidential review dossiers are not commercialized.

Legal reference used as an operating guide: Ley Federal de Protección de Datos Personales en Posesión de los Particulares. The technical document clarifies it: the legal pages are an operating starting point, not a compliance certification nor a substitute for legal review. The versioned privacy notice and ARCO rights remain outstanding for publication.
